## An association of people who live with it, in Montréal

Fibromyalgie Montréal, also called the Association de la fibromyalgie de la région de l’Île-de-Montréal, is the reference organisation for the area. It sits at 911 rue Jean-Talon Est, suite 224, and answers at 438 496-7448. Its site is [fibromyalgiemontreal.ca](https://www.fibromyalgiemontreal.ca/), in French.

What it offers, according to its own pages: support groups, a pain-management programme, workshops and talks, adapted activities such as yoga, Pilates and meditation, and documentation. Regular membership costs around thirty dollars a year and is reserved for people living with fibromyalgia; other categories exist for family members and organisations.

It is not a clinic and it does not replace medical care. Its value lies elsewhere: contact with people living the same thing, and structured self-management tools. No practitioner provides that.

## The provincial body and its resource directory

The Société québécoise de la fibromyalgie has brought the regional associations together since 2015. It answers at 450 933-6530 or 1 833 933-6533, and its site is [sqf.quebec](https://www.sqf.quebec/).

Its resource directory is the most useful document for someone starting to look: it lists the associations across Québec, along with legal and financial resources and referrals in health and mental health. It is published on the site’s [documentation page](https://sqf.quebec/documentation/).

## Living with chronic pain, beyond fibromyalgia

The Association québécoise de la douleur chronique addresses all pain that lasts, whatever its name. It runs a listening line, support groups in person and online, talks and documentation, and advocates with the authorities. Its site is [douleurquebec.ca](https://douleurquebec.ca/).

[gerermadouleur.ca](https://gerermadouleur.ca/) is the resource people know least and are most surprised by. Free, bilingual and Québec-made, it brings together the Réseau québécois de recherche sur la douleur, a teaching chair at Université Laval, Université TÉLUQ, the Association québécoise de la douleur chronique and the Société québécoise de la douleur. It hosts a self-management programme you follow at home, at your own pace.

## Talking to someone right now: 811

811 is free and answers day and night, every day of the year. Two services live there: Info-Santé for a health question, and Info-Social for psychological distress, anxiety, or simply needing to talk. The service is available in English.

Info-Social covers every region of Québec except the Cree Territory of James Bay and Nunavik. You reach it from a landline or from a mobile with a Québec number, and a relay service exists for people who are deaf or hard of hearing.

For a life-threatening emergency it is 911, not 811.

## What the public system provides for fibromyalgia

In 2021 the ministère de la Santé et des Services sociaux published a document titled *Algorithme de prise en charge de la fibromyalgie*, available from the [ministry’s publications](https://publications.msss.gouv.qc.ca/msss/document-001056/). It is in French.

Its interest for you is not medical but practical: it is the document your family physician is meant to work from. Knowing a pathway exists, and being able to name it in an appointment, changes the conversation. I do not summarise it here and I draw no conclusion from it about my own practice.
