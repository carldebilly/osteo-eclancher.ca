## A different goal

With recent pain, the body is repairing and osteopathy supports that. In a chronic situation the repair finished long ago, but the system keeps protecting an area: surrounding muscles stay tight, movements get avoided, breathing shortens. That whole structure built up around the original problem is what the work addresses.

So I am not chasing the disappearance of a symptom. I am looking with you for the concrete places where you can win back some room: sleeping better, holding a position longer, doing again something you had given up.

Osteopathy does not replace your medical care, and it does not allow me to make a diagnosis. It is added to what your care team provides.

## Pain that lasts

Chronic low back pain, neck pain, pain that moves around, the aftermath of an old accident.

What occupies the session is rarely the painful area itself but the compensations: what the rest of the body arranged in order to spare it, and which eventually costs more than the protection is worth.

## Osteoarthritis and stiffness

Joint wear does not reverse, and I will not pretend otherwise.

The stiffness around it does depend on muscles, fascia and how neighbouring joints take part in the movement. There is room on that side, and that is where my time goes, with slow, small-amplitude mobilisation.

## Headaches and recurring migraines

Many headaches are sustained by the neck, the jaw and the shoulders. We map your triggers together, and I work those regions to reduce the background discomfort.

Migraine with aura belongs with medical care. An unusual or sudden headache belongs in an emergency room, not here.

## Chronic fatigue

When energy is short the rule is simple: the session must not cost more than it gives back. Consultations are therefore shorter, further apart, and the work stays undemanding.

## Functional digestive discomfort

Bloating, irregular transit, a sense of abdominal tightness, once medical causes have been ruled out by your physician. That precaution is not a formality: these symptoms have causes that need examining, and osteopathy rules none of them out.

What remains after that is worked through the diaphragm, the abdominal wall and the mobility of the viscera.

## Anxiety and chronic stress {#anxiety}

Prolonged anxiety leaves recognisable physical traces: high, shallow breathing, raised shoulders, a clenched jaw, a locked neck, light sleep. That tension in turn feeds the sense of alertness, and the loop closes.

That is the part I can act on: giving the rib cage and diaphragm back some range, releasing what has locked up, and making you aware of signals you no longer notice.

I am neither a psychologist nor a physician, and osteopathy does not replace mental health care. If you have none and the need is there, I will encourage you to raise it with your family doctor. In Québec, the Info-Social line at 811 is available at any hour.

## When to see a physician first

Some signs call for a medical opinion before any manual approach:

- pain that appeared suddenly, very intense, with no obvious cause;
- loss of strength, spreading numbness, loss of bladder or bowel control;
- fever, unexplained weight loss or night sweats alongside the pain;
- a headache unlike your usual ones, especially one that peaks within seconds;
- a recent injury that has not been assessed.

In those situations, see your physician or call 811. If they come to light during a session here, I will stop and refer you.
